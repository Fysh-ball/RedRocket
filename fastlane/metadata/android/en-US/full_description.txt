When an emergency alert reaches your phone, Red Rocket reads it, checks it against scenarios you wrote in advance, and sends your pre-written message to the people you chose. No fumbling with your phone in the dark, no trying to remember who to call first.

There's no account and no server, and your contacts never leave the device.

<b>How it decides</b>

Red Rocket listens for emergency broadcasts through Android's cell broadcast system and its notification listener. Before it sends anything it runs eight checks in a fixed order, including a hard block on test alerts, multilingual phrase matching, and a trust check on which app the alert came from. It doesn't fire on tests.

Presets cover 14 disaster categories with detection keywords in 22 languages across 35 countries. The app picks your region from the SIM and you can override it.

<b>Scenarios and groups</b>

A scenario has activation keywords that make it fire, block phrases that cancel it (like "this is a test"), and one or more groups of contacts. Family can get one message and coworkers another. When the scenario fires, every group sends at once.

<b>Delivery that keeps trying</b>

Messages go out in parallel. If the network is struggling and sends start failing, Red Rocket drops to sequential sending, then to a retry mode that keeps going until everything lands. You can tell it to keep trying indefinitely.

<b>Replies come back</b>

After sending, Red Rocket watches for replies. Contacts answer with 1 for safe, 2 for safe but wants updates, or 3 for emergency. The dashboard shows who answered, what they said, and who hasn't answered yet.

<b>Also included</b>

- Manual send with a captcha and countdown, so it can't go off by accident
- A home screen widget showing status without opening the app
- Backup and restore of your scenarios to a file
- Optional alert enrichment. It's off by default, and when it's on it sends approximate coordinates to Event Horizon Web and nothing else, to ask what's happening within 15 km

<b>Privacy</b>

No accounts, no telemetry, no analytics. Contacts, scenarios and messages stay on the device. In a default install the only outbound request is a version check to GitHub on launch, which says nothing about you.

<b>Honesty about how it was built</b>

Red Rocket was written with AI assistance, inside a written engineering specification. The disclosure is in the repository as HOW_THIS_WAS_BUILT.md and it covers the method, the numbers you can count yourself, and where the project is weak.

Red Rocket is not a replacement for official emergency services or your country's alert system.
